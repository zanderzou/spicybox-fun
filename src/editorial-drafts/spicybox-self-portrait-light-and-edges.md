Choose the self-portrait in which your face and outline are easiest to read, rather than the one with the strongest filter. A dramatic still can hide an eye, merge hair into the background or crop away a shoulder that a moving shot would need.

**Use only an image of yourself, and only if you meet SpicyBox's adult eligibility requirements.** Its Terms of Use require you to be the person depicted and to hold the necessary rights. A friend's permission does not turn their portrait into your own self-image under that rule.

## Look for readable detail before dramatic lighting

Open two candidate photographs at the same display size. Look at both eyes, the mouth, hairline and collar. If one side of the face disappears into shadow, try a different photograph before attempting to correct it with stronger instructions.

Soft window light is one practical option. Turn slightly toward the window and use a plain background, without looking directly into strong sunlight. The aim is visible detail and comfortable contrast; this is a photography suggestion, not a documented SpicyBox setting.

The illustration shows the lighting idea, not a submitted photograph or a generated SpicyBox result. For your actual input, move a hand away from the face if it hides a feature you need to evaluate.

## Check the boundary of the subject

Trace the outline with your eyes: hair, cheek, neck, shoulder and visible clothing. A dark jacket against a dark sofa may look elegant but give you little separation to inspect. A busy patterned curtain can make the same outline harder to judge.

Leave some breathing room around your head and shoulders. Do not stretch a tightly cropped face to manufacture extra room. Retake the photograph or choose a wider original if an important edge is already missing.

## A useful two-photo comparison

Imagine two of your own fully clothed portraits. In the first, the face is sharp but a lamp crosses the shoulder. In the second, the background is plain and the eyes are clear, although the expression is less dramatic. For an initial attempt, the second gives you fewer visible ambiguities to investigate afterward.

Write down the reason for your choice: “Plain wall; visible eyes; both shoulders within frame.” Keep that note with the original file. If you later compare a different input, you can explain what changed instead of guessing from a thumbnail.

## Know when another attempt will not answer the question

SpicyBox's terms warn that generated content may contain artifacts or distortions. A clean input does not guarantee realistic motion, preserved identity or a usable result. If a feature is unclear in the source, first resolve that source problem; repeated generation cannot establish what the missing detail originally looked like.

Before uploading, choose one eligible self-portrait and identify one thing you will inspect in the result, such as whether the visible shoulder remains coherent during movement. Keep the first comparison narrow enough to make the next decision useful.
