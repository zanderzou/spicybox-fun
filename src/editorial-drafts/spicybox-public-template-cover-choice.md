Treat a public template submission as a separate sharing decision. A clip you are comfortable keeping in your account may still have a cover, title or background detail you would not choose for a public gallery.

SpicyBox's Terms of Use distinguish private templates from submissions made through the “Publish Your Template on the Platform” option. They say approved templates and their covers may become public and available to other users. Do not assume the cover remains private because the original creation began privately.

## Review the cover as a standalone image

Look at the proposed cover without playing the video. Does it convey the kind of motion or scene the template is intended to represent? Can a reader understand it at a small thumbnail size? Remove a candidate from consideration if it only makes sense after watching the whole sequence.

Use a fully clothed, non-explicit cover for the example workflow here. Keep the subject clear and avoid background text, identification documents or reflections you would not intentionally publish.

## Check the title and cover together

A neutral cover can still be paired with a revealing title. A title containing a workplace, real surname or private occasion can disclose something the image does not. Read both as an unfamiliar visitor would encounter them.

For example, “Soft window-light turn” describes a visual idea. A title that adds a person's full name and a home address contributes little to that idea while exposing much more. Use the smallest amount of personal context needed to identify the template.

Avoid titles that promise an outcome the cover cannot establish. One attractive frame does not demonstrate consistent motion, preserved facial detail or the same result for every eligible input.

## Keep the self-image rule beside the submission decision

The terms require eligible adults to upload images of themselves and to control the necessary rights. Keep other people and their likenesses out of the input. A public-template feature does not expand whose photograph you may process.

Also distinguish permission to submit an image from a belief that every element in it is yours to republish. If a poster, artwork or another person's reflection is central to the proposed cover, choose a simpler cover before submission rather than trying to resolve that question afterward.

## Save a small submission record

Keep the selected cover file, the title you chose and the date you submitted it. If the submission screen provides a review status, record that status accurately; “submitted” is not the same as “approved.” Do not infer publication from a saved preview alone.

Your final check is simple: would you intentionally show this cover and this title together to an unfamiliar visitor? If the answer depends on a detail staying unnoticed, revise the submission before continuing.
